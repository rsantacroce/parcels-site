Money is shown in dollars. The sim counts in cents per tick; a month is 30 ticks.

A game tick

Every tick runs nine stages in order, so a replay always reproduces the same city:

  1. Commands: everything you asked for is applied, in order.
  2. Utilities: power and water networks are flood-filled and each network serves its tiles from its own supply. Power is settled before water, because pumps need power.
  3. Demand: residential, commercial, industrial and office demand, shifted by your tax rate.
  4. Growth: every eligible zone tile competes for one shared pool of newcomers.
  5. Land value: service coverage, pollution and crime are recomputed and land value moves toward its target.
  6. Commute (every 5 ticks): residents route along the roads to jobs, which sets each road's traffic, bus ridership and fares.
  7. Economy: taxes and bus fares in, upkeep out, treasury clamped at the debt floor.
  8. Status: residents score nine parts of city life and approval drifts toward the total plus goodwill.
  9. Mayor: open letters are granted or lapse; once a month goodwill fades, a resident may write in, and on election day the city votes.

There is no time limit: the city runs as long as you like.

Taxes

Every powered, occupied zone tile pays tax every tick:

tax per tick (¢) = occupants × land value × tax rate % ÷ 1500
tax per month = tax per tick × 30
  • Occupants = level × people (or jobs) per level of that zone. See Zones.
  • Industry is taxed at base land value (200) wherever it stands: factories don't care about the view. Every other zone pays on its tile's actual land value.
  • Unpowered tiles pay nothing.
  • Tax rate runs from 0 to 20%, default 7%. Each point above 7% cuts demand and costs approval (see Demand), each point below raises it.

A useful rule of thumb at 7%: each occupant pays a month per 100 points of land value.

Power and water

Each connected network adds up its supply and serves its tiles in order, water producers first. When demand outstrips supply, the remaining tiles go without and count as unserved.

  • Power use: a zone tile needs 1 + its level; a building needs its listed power draw.
  • Water use: a zone tile needs its level (empty lots need none). Levels above 1 won't grow without water.
  • Water producers make nothing without power. Pumps and treatment plants that touch the river get their river bonus. Pipes can follow a bridge across water.

Regions

  • A link exists on an edge when any tile on that edge has a road (road link), a power line (power) or a water pipe (water), and there's a city beyond it.
  • Road link: the neighbour's open jobs (workers its jobs could still take) wait at your edge roads for commuters, and 50% of its job seekers drive in to your open jobs. Raw demand rises by 25% of its open jobs (homes), of its population ÷ 300 × 100 (shops) and of its job seekers (factories). Its open jobs also count against your jobless rate.
  • Power or water link: a network touching the edge buys up to the neighbour's spare when it's short, at 150¢ per 100 units per tick, and sells its spare to a short neighbour at 80¢.
  • Neighbours are snapshots taken when each city was last saved, sent to the city by a command when you open it, so replays reproduce them.

Commuting and buses

  • 50% of residents travel to work. Each job takes 130/100 workers, and its workers arrive at the road nearest the job.
  • Commuters join the road within 2 tiles of home that gets them to work cheapest. Crossing a street costs 4, an avenue 3; ties break on map position, so the result is the same on every machine.
  • Nearer homes claim jobs first. When every nearby job is taken, the rest search again farther out, up to 16 rounds. A home with no road route to any job counts as out of work.
  • A road's traffic is the trips crossing it × 40 ÷ its capacity (street 150, avenue 360). The commute time shown is route cost ÷ 4 + 2 minutes.
  • Buses. A bus stop must touch a road and covers 4 tiles. It works when a powered bus depot touches the same road network. When both the home and the job's road are covered, 40% of those trips go by bus, up to 600 riders per depot. Riders pay a month each.

Letters and elections

  • Each month there's a 500‰ chance a resident writes in, once the city has 60 people, with at most 3 letters open and never two asking for the same thing.
  • 70% of letters are about the top issue; the rest pick a factor at random. A letter is only sent if there's something concrete to ask for that isn't already done.
  • A letter lasts 12 months. Granted: goodwill +60 (tenths of a percent of approval). Lapsed, or the writer's home is gone: −40. Goodwill is capped at ±200 and fades by 8% a month.
  • Elections every 48 months; you win with 50% approval or more.
residential demand (‰) += (approval − 50%, in tenths of a percent) × 40 ÷ 100

So at 80% approval residential demand gets +120‰, and at 20% it loses 120‰.

Demand

Raw demand is the balance of residents and jobs, normalised to −1000…1000:

region = 100% + 30% per game year
R = 80×region + bonuses + (all jobs incl. public) × 130/100 − residents
C = 20×region + bonuses + residents × 100/300 − commercial jobs
I = 80×region + bonuses + residents × 100/220 − industrial jobs
O = 10×region + bonuses + residents × 100/450 − office jobs
bar = raw × 1000 ÷ (current size + 100 for R, + 50 for C/I/O)

The bars you see are the raw bars minus (your tax − 7) × 4 × 10. At 10% tax you lose 120 points on every bar; at 4% you gain 120. Bonuses come from powered landmarks (stadium, town hall, airport); see their entries.

If demand for a zone falls below −500, occupied tiles of that zone start leaving even when they are well served.

Growth

Each tick, up to 4 tiles per zone type, move up or down a level. Which ones is decided by competition:

growth score = land value + demand ÷ 4 + random 0…60
  • Only tiles that are sustained (powered, a road within 2 tiles, and watered if above level 1) can grow while demand is positive. The highest scores win, so cheap land loses newcomers to valuable land.
  • When demand is negative, the lowest-scoring occupied tiles shrink first.
  • A tile that isn't sustained loses a level with a 4% chance each tick.
